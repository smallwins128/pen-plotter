Relief prints. For E and F the print is a heightmap rather than tiles: one part whose top surface rises and falls behind the steel. Where it sits below the steel you see depth and shadow; where it rises through a cut it must be at least 0.3 mm narrower than the cut on each side, so those areas follow the cut outline exactly.

How it is made. The laser cuts every cell out of the 1 mm steel, leaving a lattice that must stay one connected piece, so closed shapes get steel ties. The print is one multi-colour part: a 1.2 mm backer sheet, with every piece rising 1 mm through its cell (0.15 mm smaller per side) and stopping flush, or standing up to 3 mm proud on the fronts. Open cells are holes in both the steel and the backer. The backer pushes in from inside and is held by VHB tape or the screws already on that face.

What the print adds. Colour changes happen in the backer, hidden under the steel, so each visible piece is one clean colour. The backer also seals the lid against dust and stiffens the cut area.

Rules every study keeps. Cut area at least 7 mm in from the folded edges and clear of the lid's fixing holes, the corner rivets and the strip under the lid's front lip. Steel webs are at least 2 mm, and every piece of steel is tied to the frame.
