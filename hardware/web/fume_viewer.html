How it holds together

  • One set of screws: , all from the front of the filter part into . The 4 fan screws pass through the fan's own corner holes. The back shows no screws.
  • Face: no screws. A 3 mm lip drops into the pad pocket, and magnets hold it on (). All 6 screw heads sit flush under the face.
  • Filters: front: pull the face off. Rear: also undo the 6 screws and lift out the filter part and the fan.
  • Handle: 2 × M3 heat-set inserts in the top, 50.8 mm apart, which matches the end holes of a 5-hole Meccano strip. Fit two M3 standoffs and screw the strip on top.
  • Base: the rear pad sits between 4 solid corner tabs that stop at the top of the pad. The fan sits on the tab tops, which hold the inserts, and the screws through its holes locate it. The 2 chin tabs run the full height of the base, with their inserts in its front face.
  • Grilles: each one is a small separate print. A plate shaped like the pocket sits behind the panel, and a boss fills the Ø74 hole flush with the outside. The pad behind it pins it in place, so it needs no glue or clips. The octagon plate keeps slots from turning. Print variants and swap them any time.
  • Pads: cut each one 80 mm square and snip 16 mm off every corner, which clears the screws.
  • Wiring: the fan lead drops into the hollow chin and runs to the jack (Ø12) and the switch (Ø6, with a 15 mm body), side by side in the back panel's chin. The cable leaves from the back.
  • Mounting: each side has 2 × M5 heat-set inserts in a solid block inside the chin wall, 18 mm apart in a row front to back, so a bracket can't pivot. M5 matches the T-nuts for 2020/2040 aluminium frame.
