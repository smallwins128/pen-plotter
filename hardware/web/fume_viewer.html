How it holds together

  • One set of screws: , all from the front of the filter part into . The 4 fan screws pass through the fan's own corner holes. The back shows no screws.
  • Face: no screws. A 3 mm lip drops into the pad pocket, and magnets hold it on (). The 4 fan screws sit 2 mm deep, and a magnet clings to each steel head (use zinc-plated steel screws, not stainless). The face's magnets snap onto them.
  • Filters: front: pull the face off. Rear: also pick out the 4 magnets, undo the 6 screws, and lift out the filter part and the fan.
  • Handle: 2 × M3 heat-set inserts in the top, 50.8 mm apart, which matches the end holes of a 5-hole Meccano strip. Fit two M3 standoffs and screw the strip on top.
  • Grilles: each one is a small separate print. A plate shaped like the pocket sits behind the panel, and a boss fills the Ø74 hole flush with the outside. The pad behind it pins it in place, so it needs no glue or clips. The octagon plate keeps slots from turning. Print variants and swap them any time.
  • Pads: cut each one 80 mm square and snip 16 mm off every corner, which clears the screws.
  • Wiring: the fan lead drops into the hollow chin through a notch in the body, then runs to the jack and switch, which sit side by side in the chin's right wall.

  • 8 × Ø6 × 2 magnets
  • 2 × M3 standoffs, a 5-hole Meccano strip and 2 × M3 screws
  • Panel DC jack (Ø12 hole), a switch (Ø6 hole)
  • 2 carbon pads, 9 mm thick, cut 80 mm square with 16 mm snipped off each corner. Each pocket is 10 mm deep.
