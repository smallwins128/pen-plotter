Car rack

Your 300 × 35 × 6 wood strips as shelves for Hot Wheels, three cars each. Two printed brackets per strip hang on the SKÅDIS rail system; the strip just rests in their ledges. Four sliding saddles, one at each end and two between, make three ≈90 mm bays. The strips aren't cut true, so nothing grips the wood: every pocket has 1.5 mm or more of slack. Car sizes are estimates (≈ 75 × 32 × 35); measure one with the caliper.

Front view

Two strips, one hook row apart twice (80 mm). Brackets on hooks 240 apart, so 30 mm of strip overhangs each end.

Bracket, side section

Ledge 38 inside (strip 35 + 1.5 slack each side); the front lip stands 3 mm over the strip top. The car width is an estimate.

Divider saddle

Tunnel 38.5 × 7.5 inside, loose on a rough strip; 10 mm long, slides on from the strip's end. The fin (25 tall, 2 thick) sits at one end of the tunnel so the part prints lying on it.

How it goes up

  • Hooks in two slots of one row, 240 mm apart (6 hook spaces). Brackets slide down onto them.
  • Slide four saddles onto the strip from its ends, lay the strip in the ledges, then push one to each end and set the middle two at thirds.
  • The ledge's front lip stops the strip walking forward; the bracket plate stops it going back.
  • Next strip up: two hook rows higher (80 mm). That leaves ≈ 60 mm over a strip for a ≈ 35 mm car and your fingers.

Open questions

  • How many strips? Each takes 76 mm of height; a full 56 cm SKÅDIS fits 6 if nothing else is on it.
  • Measure one car (length, width, height) and your thickest and widest strip.
  • Cars side-on (as drawn) or nose-out? Nose-out fits 5–6 per strip at ≈ 35 mm pitch and needs no dividers, but a 35 mm deep strip leaves half the car hanging.
  • Saddles loose, or with a small bump so they stay put when a car is pulled out?
