iPad sleeve

Four standard rail hooks hang behind the iPad's corners. Each takes a plate that slides down the hook's rail, like the tool plates. The plates sit behind the iPad, which rests 9 mm off the board; from the front you only see four alike corner wraps: a 2 mm wall round the edge and a 3 mm lip over the bezel. iPad 7th gen, landscape, port on the right.

drag · scroll / pinch

How it goes on

  • Four rail hooks (your standard ones): push into the slots and drop, as always.
  • Bottom plates: slide down their hooks until they sit on the rail roofs.
  • iPad: lower it onto the two ledges, back against the plates.
  • Top plates last: hold the iPad upright, slide each top plate down its hook behind the iPad until its cap lands over the top corner. Off: slide the top plates up, lift the iPad.
  • Checked in CAD: hooks on real slots; nothing clashes while the plates slide on (top plates with the iPad in) or while the iPad drops in; the iPad can't move forward, up, sideways or back.

Where it sits

  • Hooks 200 apart across (same slot heights, so left and right plates are mirror images) and 120 apart up the column. Every hook and plate body is behind the iPad.
  • All four corners look the same from the front: legs 37 mm long; side legs 4.5 mm wide past the iPad's edge (wall + lip over the bezel); bottom bars 6 mm (3 mm ledge + lip), top bars 5 mm (2 mm cap + lip).
  • The iPad sits 9.15 mm off the board; the corners stand 18.75 mm off it.
