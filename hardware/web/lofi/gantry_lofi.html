2 · Section across the front rail

The plate stands outside the rail. Its top wheels ride the rail's top face and its bottom wheels the bottom face, 59.25 apart: the plate's 2040 holes. The bar is a 2040 on edge, its end bolted to the plate's inner face over the rail. Because the bottom wheels run under the rail, the rail has to come off the deck.

3 · Top view of the plotter bay

Bar at mid-bay. Plates and motors sit outside the frame, past the table edge. Green dashes show where the pen can reach; solid green is an A3 landscape sheet.

What changes

  • Rails lifted at least 24* off the deck so the bottom wheels clear. The 4 end brackets (108.7 tall) look made for this. The 560 ends and the divider go up with them.
  • Bar ≈ 602* of 2040, cut to fit between the plates' inner faces. Neither the 700 nor a 560 fits; the spacers set the exact length.
  • The paper is 68* below the bar, so the pen reaches down through the tool plate's 97 mm slots.
  • Motors stand 41* outside the frame, front and back, and the plates hang 26* below the tabletop past its edge. Needs a clear table edge, and about 46 at the wall.

What it does to the plot

  • The plates no longer limit X: they ride outside. The head against the end rail and divider does. Along X ≈ 530* (A3 needs 450: +80).
  • Along the bar ≈ 466* (A3 needs 330: +136). The carriage's bottom wheels sit below the rail tops, so it stays between the rails.
  • The carriage plate's 59.25 wheels fit the 2040 bar, so it fits as-is.
  • The stop blocks on the rail tops would catch the top wheels. The divider becomes the hard end for the head instead.

Your call

  1. Rail lift: the ACRO end brackets, or blocks under the frame? How high do you want the frame?
  2. The table edge: anything (lip, apron, legs) within 26 below the top at the front and back? And how far is the wall?
  3. Do you have the wheels and spacers, or only the plates?
