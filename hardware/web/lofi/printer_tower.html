Printer tower

One open carcass instead of two boxes: two full-height sides, a printer shelf, a top and two bottom rails, joined with printed connectors. The two boxes shared a panel anyway, so merging them saves wood and two joints. 600 wide × 1200 tall × 500 deep. Storage bay below (488 clear), printer bay above (676 clear) for the Bambu A1.

4 panels and 2 rails, plus 1 optional rail. About 1.8 m² of ply, against about 2.4 m² for two separate boxes. 576 = 600 − 2 × 12: the shelf, top and bottom fit between the sides.

8 mm or 12 mm

  • The printer shelf stays 12 mm. It spans 576 with an 8.3 kg bed-slinger shaking it. Stiffness goes with thickness cubed, so 12 mm is about 3.4 times stiffer than 8 mm. A bouncy shelf shows up as ringing on your prints.
  • The sides and top could be 8 or 9 mm, but the connectors grip panel edges and screws bite into them. Thin edges hold screws poorly and the carcass racks more easily. Saving here is small: fewer pieces already saved more than thinner ply would.
  • One sheet of one thickness is simpler to buy and cut. All 12 mm fits on one 7 × 4 sheet.

From one 7 × 4 sheet

  • Cuts 1 and 2: rip two strips 500 wide; the third strip is about 214.
  • Each 500 strip: crosscut at 1200 and 1776: one side and one 576 × 500 piece, about 350 over.
  • Narrow strip: rip it to 100 and crosscut three 576 rails: two for the bottom, one for the optional back rail.

Check before cutting

  • Bed travel: the A1's bed slides front and back past its frame. With the printer at home, push the bed to each end and measure. The box is 500 deep and open both ends, so the bed overhangs into air; leave a gap between the tower's back and the black board.
  • Connector gap: if the printed connectors leave a gap between panels (the photo shows a few mm), the box grows by that gap. The sizes here assume panels touching. Measure your connector first, or design it for zero gap.
  • Power: the A1 draws up to 1300 W at 220 V while heating (spec sheet), so it needs its own socket, not the bench's 6 A point.
  • Anchor the top of the tower to the wall or board: the bed moving back and forth rocks a tall stack.
