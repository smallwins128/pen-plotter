How it goes together

  • Base: 4 wood strips (35 × 6, 227 long) pass through slots in both plates, 6 mm proud each side. The PSU sits on the two at the back.
  • Rail seat, every rail end: the plate carries a 12 mm surface shaped to the rail. A clamp with a boss presses the web, held by 2 × M3 countersunk into heat-set inserts outside the rail, so slot positions don't matter.
  • Rails: top (9 KV121 + a DIN switch for the board's 2-pin socket), 2 at the back facing out, 2 on the slope for prototypes (2 KV121 feeds each).
  • Sleeve: a 1.6 mm shell follows the side profile over the 63 mm bay, with a honeycomb on its back and slope faces. Each rail screws into a rib on it (M3 nut slid into the rib's channel from the PSU end, M3 × 6 pan head through any rail slot).
  • End wall: a separate flat print that drops into the shell against a 1.5 mm ledge, held there by the PSU. Its window is the PSU face less a 3 mm lip, crossed by 2 bars for the ATX board.
  • ATX board: on 4 standoffs 15 mm long, facing the bay. The PSU's cables pass through the open window (the 74 mm band below the board, or behind it) and plug into the 24-pin socket from the bay.
  • PSU: its back face (fan, mains socket, switch) sits in the right plate's window, held by its own 4 screws in the corner pads.
