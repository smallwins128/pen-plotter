Rebuild the long stepper cable

The 1.75 m run was starving one coil — one motor jittered instead of turning. Rebuild it as a single, twisted, correctly-ordered cable and the jitter is gone.

Rule #1

Copy the working axis exactly. Wire the new cable's color → pin order identically to the axis that already jogs clean. That single habit removes every coil-pairing mistake that caused the jitter.

Parts to buy

  • cable 4-conductor 22 AWG, shielded if available — cut to the true length you need, shorter than 1.75 m if routing allows.
  • motor end JST-XH 2.5 mm 4-pin pigtail (pre-crimped). Confirm your NEMA plug is XH 4-pin before ordering.
  • shield end Dupont 2.54 mm 4-pin pigtail (pre-crimped).
  • consumables Solder + heatshrink (per-joint and one bundle sleeve).

Build it

  1. Match the working axis. Note its color → pin order and wire the new cable identically.
  2. Solder cable to pigtails — keep each coil's two leads together, one clean unbroken conductor per wire.
  3. Twist each pair (A1+A2, B1+B2) along the whole run to reject noise.
  4. Heatshrink every joint, then sleeve the bundle. No loose twists.
  5. Strain-relief both ends — anchor or cable chain so gantry motion never flexes a solder joint.

Verify before power

  • Each motor-end pin runs straight through to the same shield-end pin — probe pin-to-pin, confirm nothing is crossed.
  • Each coil pair reads ~2–4 Ω; a lead from coil A to coil B reads open.
  • If shielded: tie the shield to GND at the shield end only (leave motor end floating).

Don't chain Dupont jumper wires for the run — that is the exact thing that caused the jitter.

Alt fix — drive Y with the TB6600

Its opto-isolated inputs and current headroom shrug off a long, noisy cable — often killing the jitter on their own. Keep X on the shield; route only Y to the TB6600. Pull the Y A4988 out (GRBL still outputs Y step/dir on D3/D6).

Ground

Tie all grounds together. Shield GND, TB6600 PUL−/DIR−, TB6600 power GND, and the SMPS negative must be one common ground — that's the thick line. Skip it and the opto inputs float and won't step. This is the #1 mixed-driver mistake.

  • DIP switches: current ≈ 0.8 A (Ender motors are 0.84 A rated — don't exceed); microstep 1/8 or 1/16.
  • ENA left unconnected = driver always enabled. Fine to start.
  • Now X and Y have different microstepping → recalibrate $100 / $101 separately (jog 100 mm, measure, correct).
Then: power OFF → plug in → power ON → jog at 100 mm/min. It should move clean.
