Three ways to dress the windows

The steel is fixed: the two openings and four Ø3.4 holes round each. The acrylic and anything printed are free. Each idea works on the control-front window the same way.

A · Floating slab

  • 4–5 mm smoked acrylic, 10 mm bigger than the opening, rounded corners, edges polished. It stands 3 mm off the steel on printed spacers, so it casts a shadow and the edges catch light.
  • No bezel. Screws go in from the front through the acrylic and the steel, with nyloc nuts inside, under black cap heads.
  • Edge-lit later: LED strip inside behind the gap makes the acrylic edges glow.

B · Registration marks

  • Smoked acrylic in a thin 4 mm black bezel. The vendor laser-engraves plotter registration marks into the back: corner crop marks, a centre target and a millimetre tick scale along the bottom.
  • It reads as "a machine that draws" without a logo. Engraving is one more layer in the same cut file.
  • The engraving glows white when the inside is lit.

C · Slatted window

  • The printed bezel carries three thin fins across each opening, the same rhythm as the vent rows, so the window reads as part of the grille.
  • Clear acrylic behind. All the character is in the print, so it is easy to change later.

Mix and match works too: A with B's engraving, or C's fins on a smoked slab. Every one fits the holes already cut.
